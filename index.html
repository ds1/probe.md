PROBE.md

The Claude skill that examines and sharpens concepts.

Six-lens Socratic analysis for Claude Code. Use Probe to have agents sharpen ideas at any stage, like when your thinking is still raw and messy or after each draft is written. It runs six critical lenses in parallel and synthesizes an actionable verdict. From ds1/socratic-probes, by @ds1.

Pressure-test a decision before you lock it.

The plugin gives you versioned, updatable commands, namespaced as /socratic-probes:probe-start. The Bash and PowerShell one-liners instead copy the eight commands into ~/.claude/commands with bare names like /probe-start. Either way, run the command below.

/probe-start <path-to-document> <output-directory>

Six agents read your document, each applies one Socratic lens, and a seventh step consolidates everything into socratic-synthesis.md: an executive verdict, an assumption risk matrix, the alternatives the doc skipped, and a validate-before-proceeding checklist.

The six lenses

Each runs as an independent agent with no shared context, so findings do not converge prematurely.

01

Clarify Thinking

"What do you mean by...? What is the source of this idea?"

Pins down vague terms, traces where conclusions came from, and exposes reasoning chains that skip a step.

02

Challenge Assumptions

"What are you assuming? What if you were wrong?"

Surfaces the hidden premises the argument rests on and tests whether the conclusion survives them being false.

03

Evidence Basis

"What supports this? What would disprove it?"

Audits every claim for its source, flags the unsourced and the self-contradicting, and names what is missing.

04

Alternative Viewpoints

"What is the counter-argument? Who would disagree?"

Steel-mans the options the document left off the table and voices the stakeholders it did not consult.

05

Implications

"What follows from this? What are the long-term effects?"

Traces first and second-order consequences, including the cost of the recommendation turning out to be wrong.

06

Question the Question

"Is this the right question to be asking?"

Checks the framing itself, whether a prior question should be settled first, and whether the scope is set correctly.

The decision-probe loop

The lenses are the engine. The real power is running them as a disciplined loop around a decision you are about to lock. Point it at whatever you have, a page of raw notes or a finished proposal. A two-minute probe is cheap next to the cost of locking a wrong architectural choice and discovering it after you have built on top of it.

  1. Scan for an existing decision first

    Search your decision log for the nouns of the capability. If a prior decision already owns the ground, your draft is an amendment to it, not a peer.

  2. Draft it, or bring raw notes

    Have a draft? Lead with what is blocked and how reversible each option is, and anchor every number to its source. Still messy? Point the probe at your rough notes and let it sharpen the thinking before you commit to a shape.

  3. Ground the agents in the code

    When the doc cites function names or constants, tell each agent to verify against the actual code. A real probe caught a doc claiming a 3 * X multiplier where the code used 4 * X, a bug author review had read straight past.

  4. Read the synthesis cold

    Write your concessions and your pushback before touching the original, then respond once with a unified summary rather than three fragments.

  5. Handle what it found

    Load-bearing gaps become a v2 doc. A thin result gets escalated, not re-probed. Before a paid dependency, scan for a shape-similar prior decision whose constraint should carry over.

Probe when a decision changes a schema, a money flow, a cross-system contract, or locks a dependency. Skip it for anything reversible in under a day.

See it work

A complete run against a realistic proposal (adopt a paid feature-flag SaaS). It looks reasonable on a first read; the probe finds the bundled requirement, the undated price, the "in-house is free" comparison it contradicts in its own next sentence, and the alternatives it never listed.

Every command

Run the full probe, or reach for a single lens when you already know what you want to pressure-test.

/probe-startLaunch all six lenses in parallel and synthesize the findings
/probe-clarifyClarify thinking and trace the origin of ideas

/probe-evidenceExamine evidence quality and sources
